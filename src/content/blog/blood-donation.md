## Postpartum Hemorrhage: The Critical Threat

Across Sub-Saharan Africa, childbirth remains one of the leading causes of death for young women. Among all potential obstetric emergencies, **postpartum hemorrhage (PPH)** — severe, uncontrolled bleeding after giving birth — is the single most common and deadly threat, accounting for over **30% of maternal deaths**.

A mother experiencing severe postpartum bleeding can lose up to a liter of blood in minutes. If blood transfusion services are delayed due to stock shortages at regional clinics, the results are almost always fatal. Access to safe, stored blood is the definitive dividing line between life and death during childbirth.

## The Chronic Shortage in Regional Blood Banks

In Ghana, regional hospital blood banks face a persistent, critical shortage. Most blood supplies are gathered through emergency family replacement systems rather than regular, voluntary donations. This system places an enormous burden on grieving and panicked families, who must find matching donors in real time during a medical emergency.

To combat this, MabEcare Foundation coordinates regular, voluntary community blood drives to keep local clinical reserves healthy:

- **Proactive Stocking** — Keeping blood banks supplied *before* an emergency happens, ensuring blood is screened and ready at the first sign of complications.
- **Reducing Financial Strain** — Eliminating the high emergency sourcing fees families must pay when searching for commercial blood donors.
- **Community Education** — Demystifying blood donation, busting common myths, and comforting first-time donors.

## Debunking Common Blood Donation Myths

Fear and misinformation are the largest hurdles we encounter when mobilizing blood donors. Let us address three of the most persistent myths:

### Myth 1: "Donating blood makes you physically weak forever."
**The Truth:** Your body is incredibly resilient. It replaces the lost blood volume within **24 to 48 hours**, and fully restores red blood cells within 4 to 8 weeks. Most donors feel completely normal after a brief rest and a light snack.

### Myth 2: "I might contract an illness during the donation process."
**The Truth:** Your safety is paramount. Every single needle, tube, and blood bag used during a donation is 100% sterile, used once, and immediately disposed of. There is absolutely zero risk of contracting infections from donating.

### Myth 3: "My blood type is very common, so it is not needed."
**The Truth:** Common blood types are actually the ones in **highest demand**! Because most patients share these common types, clinical reserves of O+, A+, and B+ blood are depleted first during large-scale emergencies.
