The MabEcare team brought this story for Sickle Cell Disease Awareness Month. In the video, a woman tells us in her own words what it was like to find out that the girl in her care has sickle cell disease.

## When the pain started

The girl has been in her care since she was 3 years old. The woman got a call from the village saying the girl was not feeling well. Later, when the girl was back with her, she began to complain of pain in her joints again.

At first, the woman did not know what was wrong. She prayed for her and gave her medication, but the pain got worse. She carried the girl to the hospital, which is far from the roadside. The girl was given first aid, but the pain came back about 15 minutes later. After tests, the girl was admitted.

## Learning the diagnosis

At the hospital, the nurses were not telling her anything, and she was worried. A doctor asked her to stay calm, then asked if she knew anything about sickle cell disease. She did not. The doctor explained it and gave her guidelines to follow.

She says hearing the diagnosis was heartbreaking. She felt it was the end of the girl's life, and she was very sad. She has been following the guidelines for some time now, and she says the girl has been helped.

## What others around her believed

She says relatives in the village saw the illness as a spiritual matter. They suggested tying the girl's joints and making marks on her skin to ease the pain. She says they did these things, but the pain was still there.

She feels an organisation should come in to educate people, because her explaining alone may not bring understanding.

## What she wants others to know

She says people should check their genotype and blood group before getting married or having children, to know if they are compatible.

She also believes these tests should be part of care during pregnancy and just after birth, so children can be tested. She wants children to be educated about this early, so they are not shy to ask questions when they grow up.
